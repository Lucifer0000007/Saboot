# Saboot: a disaster crop-loss evidence layer

**For a district or state revenue and disaster-management department.** A proposal for a pilot. `[TO FILL]` marks what the
department decides. No figures are quoted, because none exist yet: Saboot has only run as a simulation with synthetic data.

## The problem

After a flood, evidence of crop loss fades as the water recedes, while assessment depends on officers reaching every field with
limited staff. Farmers are often not told when the clock starts, or what to capture
{{VERIFY: that assessments after floods are usually done by officials visiting fields once the water has receded, and the typical delay between the event and the assessed list — the state revenue department's assessment manual or order and past assessment timelines}}.

## What Saboot does

1. Watches rainfall, river or dam levels and satellite radar against deterministic rules over registered plots.
2. Starts a clock at the **earliest sign of the event** and calls the farmer in their language while the water is still there. The
   "capture soon" nudge is advice, not a deadline.
3. Guides three geo-tagged photos that work offline and seals them so any edit shows. A helper (sarpanch, CSC operator,
   cooperative or extension worker) can capture for several farmers, each with their own consent.
4. Gives the officer a **ranked worklist** that says why each plot is where it is, and a **draft assessment list** village by
   village, with its evidence and its uncertainty.
5. Lets a farmer who disagrees with a published entry attach their sealed evidence during the objection window.

## What Saboot does not do

It does not decide any claim, eligibility or amount, and shows no amount. It does not file anything, place real calls, or connect
to any government system. The decision happens outside Saboot, and every draft list says so.

## What the department keeps

Which plots and villages are in scope; the thresholds and parameters (labelled scenario settings until the department sets them);
whether and when a list is published, and through which process; every decision on every entry and objection; the data and its
retention; the right to stop the pilot at any time.

## What we need from the department

A plot register for the pilot villages and village boundaries; the rain and river gauge data it already uses, with danger levels;
the existing assessment forms and timelines; a nodal officer; named officers and helpers; past assessed lists to check detection
against. Details: `docs/PILOT-PLAN.md`. **[TO FILL]**

## Where it stands

A working simulation in English, Hindi and Punjabi; an open evidence format with an independent verifier; a validation harness
that has **not yet been run on real data**; a privacy and consent design that is **not legally reviewed**. Seven anonymous phone
conversations in five states (a patwari, an insurer's loss assessor, four farmers, a CSC operator), listed in
`docs/INTERVIEWS.md`; none yet in Haryana, no letter of interest and no user. A first step that needs no department data:
`docs/PILOT-PLAN.md`, "Phase 0".

## Commercial model (a proposal, no figures)

A licence for the assessment layer, plus a fee per flood event it is used for. **Farmers pay nothing.** Insurers could come
later, as a second use of the same evidence, under their own agreement. Amounts and terms are for discussion: **[TO FILL]**.
