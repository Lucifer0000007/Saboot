# Stakeholder interview guide

Purpose: learn how assessment and reporting really work before anyone builds or promises anything, and find out where the
simulation is wrong. You are not selling. Listen for what people do today, what goes wrong, and what they would never accept.
Do not describe Saboot as deciding, filing or integrating with anything: it does none of these.

## Before you start

- Ask for **30 to 45 minutes**. Tell the person who you are, why you are asking, and that they may skip any question or stop.
- Read this consent line aloud, in their language, and note the answer:

  > "I would like to note what you say, and with your permission to record it. I will quote you only if you agree, and I will
  > not put your name on it unless you say so. You can ask me to delete my notes or the recording at any time. Is that all right?"

  Recording: yes / no. Quoting with name: yes / no. Quoting without name: yes / no.
- Do not collect anyone's phone number, land details or personal data beyond what the table at the end needs.
- Mark every real-world fact you hear as **to check** until a second source agrees.

## 1. A revenue officer or patwari

1. Walk me through the last flood: when did you first hear, who told you, and what did you do first?
2. How do you decide which villages and fields to visit first? What do you wish you knew at that moment?
3. How do you record what you see? On what form, in what order, and who checks it?
4. How long from the event to the day a list is published? What takes the time?
5. What evidence do you ask farmers for? What do they usually bring? What would make an objection easier or harder to handle?
6. Who can change a list after it is published? How do objections work, and how long do you give for them?
7. If you had a ranked list that said why each plot is ranked where it is, what would make you trust it, or ignore it?
8. If a farmer sent sealed photos with a time and a rough place, what would you do with them? What would worry you?
9. What data do you already have: rain and river gauges, dam release information, plot registers? Who owns each, and how
   would we ask for access?
10. What must a tool **never** do in your work?

## 2. A KVK or university extension scientist

1. Which crops are most exposed to flooding in this area, in which weeks, and how long can they stand in water?
2. Paddy: when is it flooded on purpose, and how would you tell irrigation water from flood water on a photo or a satellite
   image? What does the crop calendar say?
3. Tall crops such as sugarcane and maize: what do you see after a flood that a photo should capture? What can a satellite miss?
4. When do crop-loss signs fade, and what is the best time to take a photo?
5. What guidance do you give farmers on what to photograph? What would you add to a three-photo list?
6. How do you currently judge damage share? What is the right way to say "uncertain"?
7. Which public data (rain, river, satellite) would you trust, and which would you not?

## 3. A sarpanch or farmer group

1. When was the last flood? How did you find out it was coming, and how did the village react?
2. Who in the village helps others with forms, photos or phones? How many people do they help in a day?
3. What happens when the signal is weak or absent? Where do people go to get online?
4. Which language do people prefer for a voice call? Would they answer an unknown number?
5. If someone took photos of your field, what would you want to be told first, and who may see them?
6. What would stop you from agreeing? What would make you trust it?
7. What happens today when someone disagrees with a list? Who listens?
8. Do some farmers have only a basic phone? How do they usually get messages?

## 4. An insurer claims person

(Only for the second rail.)

1. After a localized calamity, how do you first learn that farmers are affected, and what do you need to start a claim?
2. What evidence is rejected most often, and why? What makes you accept photos?
3. How do you treat late intimation? Who decides, and on what basis?
4. What would you need to see in an evidence packet to trust it? What would you never accept?
5. Who sends you the farmer's report today, and what is lost between the farmer and you?
6. What would you never want a third party to do on your behalf?

## Close

Ask: "What should I have asked, and did not? Who else should I talk to?" Thank them, and say what will happen to their notes.
